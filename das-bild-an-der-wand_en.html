The Picture on the Wall

What the Ludwig Erhard Zentrum in Fürth shows and what it leaves out

A single picture hung in Ludwig Erhard’s study. It showed his teacher, Franz Oppenheimer. Since 2018 the town of Fürth has had a house for Erhard with 1,400 square metres of exhibition. The teacher is a marginal figure there.

I. The house

The Ludwig Erhard Zentrum has stood since 2018 next to Fürth’s town hall, opposite the small house where Erhard was born in 1897 above his parents’ linen shop. The new building is by the Munich firm Reinhard Bauer. It consists of blocks pushed one over another and is what people in Fürth call a lump. The house’s own publicity calls it a landmark.

The state did not build it. The owner and operator is a private foundation, the Stiftung Ludwig-Erhard-Haus. Its chair is the television journalist Evi Kurz, who has also led the Ludwig Erhard initiative group in Fürth since 2002. But the state paid for most of it. The federal government gave six million euros from an urban development programme. Together with the Free State of Bavaria it came to 14.5 million euros, according to Bavaria’s building minister, against estimated construction costs of 14.7 million. Bavaria covered the running costs in full until 2018. The Federal President and the Bavarian premier opened it.

So a private circle of admirers used public money to build a house for the man who regarded subsidies as an evil. There is no need to mock that. But one may ask what the money bought.

II. What you see there

The permanent exhibition covers 1,400 square metres and has more than fifty media stations. The birthplace deals with childhood, apprenticeship, war, studies and the years up to 1945. The new building follows with the economics minister, the chancellor and a “future room” with a large screen. The parents’ old shop now houses Café Luise, named after Erhard’s wife. For children there is “Ludwig’s Little World”, a learning supermarket where they may shop and work the till by the hour.

All of this is friendly and none of it is reprehensible. Teaching children how an economy works is no betrayal of Erhard. The point lies elsewhere.

From 2023 to the end of 2024 the house showed a special exhibition on Henry Kissinger, who was also born in Fürth. It was called “Henry – World Influencer No. 1”, and Kissinger opened it himself. It told mainly the story of his family. The American magazine Responsible Statecraft called its treatment of his years in power hagiographic. The chair of the foundation has made a film and written a book about the Kissinger brothers. That is how the foreign-policy maker of the bombing of Cambodia came into the house of the man who wanted to replace power with competition. The place of birth was the justification.

III. What is missing, in the judgement of specialists

One of us has walked through the house. His impression: the effort is out of proportion to the substance. So that this does not remain a mere impression, we also rely on a study made by museology students at the University of Würzburg in the winter of 2019/20. They went through the birthplace room by room and wrote down what they found.

Franz Oppenheimer, Erhard’s doctoral supervisor, does appear. He is in the room on the Weimar Republic, on a wall graphic showing Erhard’s personal connections, next to the Nuremberg market researcher Wilhelm Vershofen. The reviewers report no explanation of what Oppenheimer taught. That matches the memory of our own visit: Oppenheimer is mentioned in the birthplace, but not with the weight he had for Erhard. We add that the person judging here is a declared admirer of Oppenheimer.

Their overall verdict is harsher than ours. The exhibition, they write, shows Erhard as a model who always acted rightly. Contested interpretations do not appear. The room on the years 1933 to 1945 is staged as a clean record. Erhard was never a party member; that is true. But in those years he wrote expert reports on the economy of the Saar-Palatinate, of occupied Lorraine and of other conquered territories, and his famous memorandum of 1944 on post-war debt was written with money from the Reich Group for Industry. How to assess this is disputed among historians. The Ludwig Erhard Foundation in Bonn published its own documentation in reply in 2019. In a house paid for by the state, that dispute belonged on the wall.

IV. The teacher

An earlier version of this text needs correcting here. It read Oppenheimer as a witness against the state. That is the American reading, and it is wrong.

Oppenheimer was a doctor in the poor districts of Berlin before he became an economist. His question was why there is poverty if competition is supposed to benefit everyone. His answer: because competition was never free. The land had been distributed before it began. Whoever has no land and can get none must sell his labour at any price. Oppenheimer called this the land enclosure, the Bodensperre. His book “The State” of 1907 explains how it arose, namely through conquest. He called the appropriation of other people’s labour by force the political means, and one’s own labour and free exchange the economic means. We have set this out in “Das politische Mittel” (in German).

For him this did not lead to a weak state. It led to a programme: break up the great landed estates, found settlement cooperatives, abolish monopolies. He called it liberal socialism. He was a socialist and a Zionist, he founded cooperatives in Brandenburg and in Palestine, and the National Socialists drove him out of the country in 1938. He died in Los Angeles in 1943.

Erhard took his doctorate with him in 1925, and he tried to turn this teaching into policy. What he took over he said himself: the mistrust of monopolies and cartels and the belief in competition as a remedy against power. He swapped adjective and noun and spoke of social liberalism. For seven years he fought the industrial associations and parts of his own party for the cartel law of 1957. That law is Oppenheimer translated into a statute.

The other half was left undone. In “Prosperity for All”, Erhard’s book of 1957, Oppenheimer’s name and his concepts do not occur, nor does land. We counted this in “The Teacher Silenced by Necessity” and called it half a revolution in “Freedom and Prosperity for All”. Our reading is that Erhard kept silent so as not to endanger what could be achieved. A Jewish socialist and land reformer as chief witness would not have made the cartel law any easier in the West Germany of the 1950s. The motive cannot be proved. What is documented is that as chancellor he made up for what he had omitted as minister: the speech, the foreword, the postage stamp.

In 1965 the historian Hermann Meier-Cronemeyer wrote in the trade union monthly Gewerkschaftliche Monatshefte that Erhard’s discipleship was a legend. That is too harsh. What is true is that the pupil was able to realise only part of the teaching.

That is why the gap in Fürth is more than an oversight. Whoever shows Oppenheimer whole shows the Erhard who wanted something, and at the same time shows what remained open. A visitor who has understood this asks on the way out who owns the land on which rents are rising. He asks who today draws an income without producing anything. Our text “Plunder, Lease and Inheritance” deals with this. In a house that tells the social market economy as a finished success story, such questions are a nuisance.

V. The misunderstood miracle

The miracle itself also ran differently from the legend. In February 1950, a year and a half after the currency reform, a good two million people in West Germany were out of work. In the autumn of 1950 the Federal Republic stood on the brink of insolvency at the European Payments Union. In February 1951 Erhard’s own ministry suspended the liberalisation of imports. In 1952 a law obliged the consumer goods industry to raise one billion marks for coal, steel and energy.

What set things alight was the Korean War. The victors converted their factories to armaments. West Germany was not allowed to build weapons and supplied machinery, vehicles and steel. Exports rose from 8.4 billion marks in 1950 to 14.6 billion the following year. In 1953, in the London Agreement, the creditors waived roughly half of the foreign debt.

Free prices laid the foundation. But a European credit carried the country through the most dangerous phase, and Erhard saved his order by doing, for a while, the opposite of what he taught. That does not diminish him. The economist Ludger Lindlar called his book on the subject “The Misunderstood Economic Miracle”.

This view has been the state of research in economic history for decades. We cannot rule out that it appears somewhere in Fürth. On the walk through the exhibition it was not to be found.

VI. The brand and the man

There are two Erhards. One is a brand, the genial man with the cigar whom everyone invokes. The other wanted to break the power of the cartels and associations and fell over it as chancellor in 1966, brought down by his own party. He is the subject of “The Real Erhard”, “The Picket Fence” and “The Invisible Operation of 1966”.

According to the findings from Würzburg, the house in Fürth has opted for the brand. It exhibits a man who was always right. Such a man is good for ceremonial speeches. The other Erhard would be an indictment today, of the market power of a few corporations as much as of the associations he did not overcome. Showing him would have given the house the idea it lacks.

VII. The speech

On 30 April 1964 Erhard, then Federal Chancellor, spoke at the Free University of Berlin on the hundredth anniversary of Oppenheimer’s birth. He said that most people could not gauge how much they owed this man. He himself knew, and that was why only a single picture hung in his study. In the same year a postage stamp with Oppenheimer’s head appeared at his instigation.

A speech, a picture, a stamp. That is how Erhard honoured the man to whom he owed his thinking. His own memorial has fifty media stations.

Oppenheimer’s “The State” is available free online. The book has a good two hundred pages.

Correction of 24 November 2026. This text first appeared in February 2026 under the title “A Monstrous Monument to a Failed Hero” and has been completely revised. The earlier version said the building had cost 18 million euros. What can be documented is estimated construction costs of 14.7 million and public grants of 14.5 million. The permanent exhibition covers 1,400 square metres, not 1,200. The Kissinger exhibition was no longer current; it ended in 2024. A sentence about capitalism and communism attributed to Erhard is not in his speech of 1964 and has been deleted. The claim that museums are built only for the defeated was wrong: Adenauer, Brandt, Schmidt and Kohl have federal memorial foundations. It was also wrong that the economic means worked on its own from 1948 to 1951. Oppenheimer was not an opponent of the state but a liberal socialist. And Erhard is not a failed hero: the old title suggested a verdict on him that we do not share. The addendum of 30 October 2026 has been absorbed into section V.
