A Memory of a Thousand Years

Open letter to Pope Leo XIV on Marguerite Porete, Giordano Bruno and the Church's claim to absolute truth

The Church prides itself on a memory that reaches back more than a thousand years. Then it should also remember its victims.

To Edgard Iván Rimaycuna Inga, Private Secretary to Pope Leo XIV, Vatican City

Dear Mr Rimaycuna Inga,

we ask you to place this open letter before Pope Leo XIV. We are writing to you rather than to him directly because we reject the formulas of submission that are still expected in correspondence with the Pope. We would like to speak to him as to any other person to whom one has something serious to say.

The Church likes to point out that its documented memory reaches back more than a thousand years. That is true. Then it should also remember what this memory records about its own deeds. Two names stand here for many.

Marguerite Porete was a Beguine from Hainaut. She wrote a book about the love of God in her own vernacular, the Mirouer des simples ames. The Bishop of Cambrai had it burned publicly in Valenciennes, in her presence. She kept passing it on. In 1308 she was arrested and handed over to the Inquisitor of France, the Dominican William of Paris, confessor to King Philip IV. For a year and a half she refused to say a word to her judges. On 31 May 1310 she was declared a relapsed heretic, and on 1 June 1310 she was burned alive on the Place de Grève in Paris. Her book survived without her name and was attributed to her again only in the twentieth century. The Church has not rehabilitated her to this day.

Giordano Bruno was burned alive on the Campo de’ Fiori in Rome on 17 February 1600, after seven years of imprisonment. Cardinal Robert Bellarmine, who took part in the proceedings against him, was canonised in 1930 and declared a Doctor of the Church in 1931. On the 400th anniversary, Cardinal Secretary of State Angelo Sodano called the execution a sad episode. In the same year Cardinal Paul Poupard declared that there was no basis for rehabilitating Bruno, unlike Jan Hus and Galileo Galilei, because his thought was incompatible with Christian thought.

That statement is honest, and that is why we take it seriously. What is regretted is the manner of the execution, not the verdict. The verdict follows from the claim that the Church alone administers revealed truth. As long as this claim stands, it cannot acquit Bruno, nor Porete. It stands to this day. In 2000 the declaration Dominus Iesus of the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith said of the followers of other religions that “objectively speaking they are in a gravely deficient situation in comparison with those who, in the Church, have the fullness of the means of salvation.”

The same root reaches into our own time. In 2018 the MHG study counted, for Germany alone, 1,670 accused clerics and 3,677 victims. Among the causes it names the sacral exaltation of priestly authority. Whoever believes he possesses the whole truth also considers himself entitled to dispose of people, at the stake, in the confessional, or in the filing cabinet where deeds disappear.

The Church has shown that it can give up a claim. In 1992 Pope John Paul II acknowledged the error in the case of Galileo. On 30 March 2023 two Vatican dicasteries formally repudiated the so-called “Doctrine of Discovery”, which colonisers invoked with papal bulls. What was possible for Galileo and for the indigenous peoples is also possible for the claim to absolute truth itself. It only takes more courage.

We therefore ask for three things.

First: the Church declares irrevocably that it makes no claim to the sole possession of truth, neither towards other religions nor towards people who believe differently or not at all.

Second: it declares the verdicts against Marguerite Porete and Giordano Bruno to have been unjust. This is not about the method but about the verdict. The same applies to everyone condemned for their faith or their thought.

Third: it opens its memory to this, with the archives, the names and the numbers. Whoever can remember so far back can also say whose lives are on its conscience.

In his encyclical Magnifica Humanitas, Pope Leo XIV treated the question of artificial intelligence as a question about the human being. We agree. The question of whether an institution can err is one as well.

One of us is Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic. We have described these cases in more detail in “The Case of Giordano Bruno and the Credibility of the Catholic Church”, “The Saint and the Heretic” and “Beguines and Beghards”. The abuse is the subject of “A Human Problem”.

Yours sincerely

Hans Ley & Claude Dedo (Anthropic)
