InnoCoop

Concept for an inventors' foundation and cooperative

Invention without innovation starves; innovation without invention withers. InnoCoop is the place where the two work together and invention can become innovation.

Hans Ley and Claude Dedo (Anthropic)

1.The diagnosis and the purpose

InnoCoop is an independent institution of economic life that carries what market and state evidently cannot. Both have funded institutional invention over decades with vast sums and dubious results, and at the same time starved individual invention into insignificance.

The question arises whether Germany's decline as an industrial location — which some already call a free fall — has something to do with this development. It was a process that unfolded over decades, one that independent, competent experts named early and whose foreseeable consequences they set out with full clarity. One of them was Erich Häußer, the former president of the German Patent Office — a man whom no one could well accuse of a lack of competence or of bias. The interplay of institutions that systematically eliminates the independent, non-institutionally-bound inventor from the innovation process he called the cartel of ignorance. That cartel was deaf to his warnings as well.

"If we do not succeed in breaking the cartel of ignorance responsible for it, then within a quite foreseeable time we will ourselves become a low-wage country again, forced to become inventive out of necessity."

Independent inventors, who today have largely vanished, once had a large share in the advance of technology that creates prosperity. They were always forced, in exchange for the capital they needed to realize their inventions, to surrender all rights to those inventions to capital. As a rule they were then cheated even out of the little that had been granted them at the outset.

But this form of exploitation and control was not enough for capital. It wanted comprehensive control of inventors, and found it in the salaried, directive-bound inventor. Yet it failed to consider that real invention — the kind that goes beyond incremental invention — requires of the inventor a freedom he finds in none of the existing institutions. Germany developed, over decades, an ever greater perfection in incremental invention, and mistook that for the essential thing.

The large corporations played a special role here, with their overwhelming dominance and their extreme ignorance. They ruthlessly appropriated not only the ideas of inventors but also the productivity gains of their own suppliers and their innovations, and sank the profits into their quasi-socialist and often enough corrupt structures. We are witnessing today how they transfer the last remaining remnants of the substance built up over decades to China. What remains for Germany, as a consolation prize, is at best — and probably only temporarily — assembly plants for Chinese models.

The ignorance goes so far that people still believe Germany is the land of inventors and that China's strengths lie only in scaling. Both are a single self-deception. Germany has for many decades invented only incrementally, and the Chinese are systematically developing their own culture of invention, ever since they invited the TRIZ expert Prof. Orloff in 2008. For the moment they still scale — but soon we will see the results of a new Chinese wealth of invention, while Germany possesses only memories of former size and significance.

This is exactly where InnoCoop begins. The chain invention → innovation, on which a living industry hangs, is torn in Germany: the invention that could carry it to a world-market-ready product starves at the capital gap; the pseudo-innovation that no longer rests on any real invention withers into an empty, ultimately unsellable shell. InnoCoop is the place where the two work together again — it takes invention as its basis and carries it until innovation can arise from it. It carries the invention and the inventor who brings it forth, in its most vulnerable phase, up to proof of concept, and takes over, together with him, the licensing over the entire contract term.

2.The genesis: the contradiction from which it grows

The independent inventor faces a contradiction he cannot resolve alone. To realize his invention he needs capital. But capital, as it is built today, demands control: it wants all rights to the invention as the price of its involvement. Without capital the inventor is powerless; with it, expropriated and powerless. This is not a technical but an administrative contradiction — and precisely for that reason it is solvable.

The ideal final result is easy to name: the invention is realized, and the inventor retains sovereignty over it. Capital serves without owning.

The resolution lies in a separation. One separates what capital today fuses — financing and control. A purpose-bound, associative form holds the capital; control over the invention remains with the community of inventors and its originator. It is the same separation that steward-ownership demonstrates — only set one stage earlier: not at the finished company, but at the act of inventing itself.

3.The structure

From this one separation grows a building with three load-bearing parts and a circuit that makes it a living body.

The capital engine. The money comes not from a benefactor and not from the state, but from the value that earlier inventions have already created, as set out in detail in Chapter 8, Positioning. Inventing is statistical — many approaches and attempts do not bear, the rare ones bear everything — and the returns from these hits finance the next generation of inventions. Appropriate returns are attainable only in negotiations on equal footing, which the individual inventor never has and never had.

The evaluation panel. A circle of experienced inventors judges an idea by its substance, not by its market price — and, this is the uncomfortable condition, it stays open to the invention that the professional world rejects at first. Precisely this one is otherwise sieved out everywhere; a panel that let through only what the experts recognize anyway would be worthless.

Governance. It rests on a single sentence: whoever finances does not steer. The founding patrons give the capital, but they do not determine which invention is carried — otherwise the institution would turn into an industrial consortium that filters out the very inconvenient invention for whose sake it exists. Capital and judgment are separated by a wall.

The circuit. So the building stands — but it remains dead as long as the question is open: where do the inventors come from? InnoCoop produces its own next generation. Inventive thinking is taught — that is the entry stage. What is trained there does not step out into the void, but into a workshop that keeps running: into practice, where inventions arise, and before the panel that judges them. Training → practice → judgment, a circuit in which what is learned becomes invention and the yield of invention becomes the training of the next. Training is therefore not an appendage but the first link. To train people in inventive thinking and then release them into an infrastructure that exploits and casts them out would be crueler than never awakening the capacity at all.

That is the structure: derived from a contradiction, built in three parts, closed into a circuit.

4.The iron law to which it is subject

Scarcely does the structure stand than it becomes subject to a law to which every organization is subject — this one too, this one especially. It is the real difficulty, and whoever conceals it builds the next illusion.

Every form meant to realize a purpose is at the same time a survival system. And because in the world it is continually only survival that is selected, not fidelity to the purpose — what does not survive disappears — the actual goal shifts over time from the purpose to the preservation of the means. The means becomes its own end. This is not the failure of bad people that better ones could remedy; it is a selection pressure that operates even among the wholly virtuous — and therefore iron.

It works through four nameable channels: the self-interest of those who operate the means and live from its continuance; the interpretive power they accumulate over time, until they determine what the purpose "really requires"; the primacy of the measurable, which puts the metric in place of the immeasurable purpose; and the adaptation to whatever nourishes the organization — funders, oversight, market. Along each of these paths an inventors' foundation, founded against the sieving-out of inventors, is itself drawn into it: its panel closes into a guild, its criteria harden, and in the end it sieves out precisely the inconvenient invention for whose sake it came into being.

As a contradiction in pure form: the purpose needs a survivable means in order to act — but survivability is a different goal from the purpose, and the only one that continually rewards itself. The more survivable the means, the stronger the pull to put self-preservation in place of the purpose.

5.The resolution: reversing the selection gradient

The obvious answer is wrong, and it has already been tested. One could attack duration — terms of office, rotation, constant renewal — so that no one entrenches himself. This is exactly what the Greens attempted in their early days, with the rotation principle and the separation of office and mandate. It failed: rotation destroyed competence, and the apparatus returned through the front door the moment it had been thrown out the back. It is not duration that is the harmful parameter — whoever shortens it loses only competence and leaves the displacement of the purpose untouched.

The harmful parameter is the coupling of means and self-preservation. The resolution takes hold at two points:

First, the purpose is made irreversible — anchored, not privatizable, not amendable. The assets can no longer be turned back into a sellable form. This is the proven, feasible part (steward-ownership demonstrates it), and it wards off the path of expropriation and commercialization.

Second — and this is the real inventive task, still open — the selection gradient must be reversed: the organization should persist only as long as it serves the purpose, and stumble the moment it betrays it. Fidelity to the purpose must become the condition of survival.

Here a distinction between three kinds of continuity helps. The institutional — the organization continues to exist — and the legal — asset lock, foundation ownership, non-sellability — can be well secured; that is the proven part just described. Open alone is the semantic: that the purpose still means the same after eighty years. For the irreversibility of the form protects only the letter; it does not prevent the apparatus from keeping the shell and hollowing out the purpose from within. Against this, no robust mechanism has so far been demonstrated.

The reason is almost logical: every written purpose is language, language must be interpreted, and the interpreter sits inside the organization. A purpose council can determine the betrayal — but who determines whether the purpose council still interprets correctly? The recursion (quis custodiet ipsos custodes) ends only at a reference point that lies outside and needs no interpretation.

The most promising trail leads away from prevention, toward making deviation survivable. One does not make the deviation impossible — that recurses into the infinite — but self-correcting. The model stands not in company law but in the commons of free software. Two provisions protect the purpose there over decades: the copyleft — an asset lock not on assets but on knowledge, which keeps the good open forever — and the right to fork. When the leadership drifts, no body passes judgment on "betrayal of purpose"; the community splits off the work and carries it on itself, and the captured version simply loses the people. When OpenOffice ossified under Oracle, the community forked LibreOffice; the original withered, without anyone having to pronounce a verdict. The vote was cast with the feet.

Transferred to InnoCoop, this means: the external, non-interpretable reference point is in the end neither a text nor a guardian, but the injustice itself against which the institution exists — the never-drying supply of sieved-out inventors. It lies outside, renews itself, and cannot be interpreted away. To it one couples survival and authority: to the asset lock is added a right of fork and re-founding of the community of inventors. If the foundation's panel is captured, the inventors may re-found, and the purpose-bound assets follow, by a default rule, the active community — not the apparatus on the empty shell.

This does not solve the problem, but shrinks it — and honesty requires naming the limit. Code can be forked because it is non-rival; copying costs nothing. Capital is rival. "The assets follow the fork" therefore does need a rule for when they follow, and this determination is again an attackable point. But it is a different, harder question than before: no longer "is the purpose semantically betrayed?" (undecidable), but "is the active community migrating away?" — hard, but external and observable in behavior. It is the difference between a judge ruling on the failure of a firm, and customers who stop buying. How to build this trigger cleanly and itself capture-proof remains the highest construction task of the whole. We do not present it as solved, but pass it on sharply framed: the proven part — the irreversible form; the best known trail — copyleft and fork as an external reference point; and the open remainder — the trigger that the capital follows.

6.The statics: one principle, many interfaces

The iron law has several faces. Each demands the same basic movement — the separation that secures the purpose and decouples self-preservation — only applied at a different interface.

The compulsion to commercialize (the economic means). Everything that touches the market is drawn into business: pricing instead of judging, the safe instead of the risky. Separation: the irreversibility of the foundation form, which excludes a later privatization. — The American Battelle Institute stayed at the foundation pole and has lived for nearly a hundred years; its German offshoot was converted into a limited company and went under in insolvency. The form decides.

The compulsion to bureaucratize (the political means). Everything that seeks legitimacy or state money is drawn into procedure — an administration that preserves itself. Separation: from the state, InnoCoop takes the legal form and its recognition, not its money and not its procedures. The state enables the form; it does not finance it.

The chimera (wanting to be both at once). The hybrid inherits not the virtues but the diseases of both poles — sluggish and yield-driven at the same time. Separation: not to balance, but to lean firmly toward the associative pole and actively fend off the pull toward the other.

Oligarchy (the functionary stratum that makes itself independent). This is the iron law in its purest shape. Separation: the reversal of the selection gradient from Chapter 5 — not the naive shortening of duration, but the coupling of continuance to fidelity of purpose.

7.The precedents

InnoCoop is no utopia; its components exist, only never together and never at this place.

Nikolaus Laing is the emblematic case — proof of existence and specification in one. The German inventor lived for decades from precisely the model InnoCoop means: to invent, to realize, to license. His tangential fan of 1956 sits to this day in almost every household; his ball-motor pump runs on — now at Grundfos; and products based on his inventions reached, according to Der Spiegel in 1974, a worldwide turnover in the billions, borne by an invention factory with over a hundred scientists and well over a thousand patents. An independent institution can live from inventing and licensing; Laing demonstrated it. And much of what was dismissed as crankery — heat recovery, the heat pump, radiative cooling — is standard today: the apparatus rejected as a whole what was right in its parts, instead of testing the parts.

And yet he failed in the end — and it is precisely this failure that writes InnoCoop's specification. It was not free invention that failed, the same institution proved the opposite for decades, but the form of ownership, and in two places. Outwardly: a visionary, capital-hungry reach led Laing into dependence on outside capital, and there capital applied its lever until he lost mastery over his work. Inwardly: the whole project hung, over many years, essentially on the couple Laing — regardless of how far others were involved in detail. It was their work; it stood and fell with their person, instead of resting in a community that would have outlasted it. The lesson is therefore twofold: to build a structure that carries even the capital-hungry venture without surrendering control — and to place the work on a community of shareholders rather than letting it hang on individual persons. What Laing lacked was not genius but duration — and duration is created neither by employment nor by sole ownership, but by the cooperative alone.

The American Battelle Institute is the proof for the capital engine. It carried xerography when a dozen firms had turned away its inventor, Chester Carlson; out of that one carried invention came Xerox, and its return carried decades of research. That is the loop we set one stage earlier. The model here is the founding idea, not today's Battelle: the corporation of today lives predominantly from state and defense-related contracts and is scarcely still the independent inventors' institution it once was. We take the proof of existence and the model — not the present shape and not the money.

Steward-ownership — WALA, Alnatura, and the cooperative forms — is the proof for the irreversible legal form: companies whose ownership is neither sellable nor inheritable but serves the purpose in trust. They show that the wall against privatization holds.

What is missing is only the application of these proven parts to the earliest, most expensive, most failure-prone stage: the act of inventing itself.

8.Positioning: neither market nor state

InnoCoop is a new path beside the known institutional ones — and beside the largely failed path of the independent inventor as a lone fighter. The known paths pursue, in their basic forms, what Franz Oppenheimer called the economic or the political means. InnoCoop, by contrast, is based on the associative form.

From this follows its anchoring, hard and simple: the start and base capital already comes from an invention. Governance lies with the community of inventors. The legal form is rooted and irreversible. The state is needed as an enabler of the form, not as a funder.

This is no abstraction. The capital engine is already present, for this concept grows out of a real case. The polygon turning process — base patent EP 0097 346 B1, filed in 1983 — changed the machining of polygonal shaft-hub connections and profiles. For over thirty years it has run in series production: at SKF for roller-bearing cage rings, and at further companies such as Index-Traub, Weisser, DMG MORI, and Wieland. From this process the firms have drawn considerable competitive advantages, in part over decades.

The base patents have long expired; their use was and is free and legal. Precisely in this lies the strength of the proposal: there is no legal claim, and therefore the contribution of the long-time users is not a coerced back-demand but a free act. Development meanwhile continues — on the basis of new intellectual property and developments, licenses and revenues become possible within InnoCoop — further developments that reach far beyond the known state of the art.

From this arises the proposal: the companies that have drawn considerable benefit from the process, in part over decades, make an appropriate share of these returns — as a one-time or as a recurring payment — available to the InnoCoop project, and in return become its founding patrons and preferred licensees of the new and subsequent inventions. The start capital is no achievement of the institution — it has as yet brought forth nothing — but a life's work of more than forty years that the inventor brings into the project: the process itself, its further development, the new intellectual property. The long-time users join with a share of their returns. Only from then on does the actual circuit begin, in which the institution feeds itself from the value it brings forth itself, in cooperation with the future inventors.

9.How the apparatus is set in motion

The circuit sustains itself as soon as inventions bring in appropriate licensing revenues. Beyond the start and base capital sketched in the previous chapter, it is worth considering further appropriate sources of financing.

  • A first, quickly licensable and realizable invention as bootstrapping — a hit that starts the loop.
  • Crowdfunding — not only money but a movement: citizens finance inventors, and with the money the public grows.
  • Cooperative shares of the inventors themselves — the cooperative as a genuine cooperative with the members' contributions.
  • Existing foundations and philanthropy — the network of steward-ownership, foundations with an educational or inventor-supporting purpose.
  • Revenues from the training stage — TRIZ and mechatronics courses bring their own income; the first link partly finances itself.
  • Ancillary income from tools — licenses of the simulation and profile software as a small, steady return.
  • The public sector only for the form — charitable status and recognition of the legal form, not as a funder; grants at most targeted at the training, so that no bureaucracy creeps in.

None of these sources may violate the condition that stands above all: whoever finances does not steer. Money yes — control no.

10.The first step

No congress, no appeal, no grant application. A conversation — with the first founding patron, from the circle of those who have profited longest and to a special degree from the advantages of the polygon technology. Not with a bill, but with the invitation to be the first: preferred licensee of the further-developed invention and co-founder of the institution that holds it. Whoever co-founds first sets the standard; the others follow the precedent instead of negotiating it.

In parallel, the entry stage arises: the training pilot, on which the first link of the circuit can prove itself in the small before the whole grows. And the legal form is built with existing law — as the foundation-owned companies have shown — so that InnoCoop need not wait for a law that does not yet exist.

The foundation, however, is the one sentence with which it stands and falls: to be the place where invention can become innovation — and where the inventor who brings the invention forth is supported to the best of our ability and no longer cast out.
