The Competent Committees

Addendum to “We Are a Different Bank Now”: What Christian Sewing Says in Handelsblatt

One day after the leak, the head of Deutsche Bank gave his first interview. In it he refutes accusations nobody has made and confirms the one that matters.

I. The sentence

We called our text of 17 November “We Are a Different Bank Now”. A spokesman for the bank had used the sentence in 2020 after the FinCEN Files, and the bank repeated it in its statement on the leak. In his interview with Handelsblatt, published online on the evening of 17 November and in print on 18 November, Christian Sewing says it twice: “We are a different bank today” and later “We are now a different bank”.

What that is supposed to mean he explains as follows: the matters date back “for the most part many years”. Yet the most important of them fall within his own tenure. He has been chief executive since April 2018. Signa Prime was taken on in November 2019, Project Cali ran in 2019 and 2020, the expansion of the Russia business was decided in 2021. The bank he distances himself from was already his.

II. What he denies

The interviewers asked whether he had ever overridden a negative decision of the risk committee. Sewing replied that in eight years and nine months he had “personally” never overruled a decision of the risk committee. And immediately afterwards: “That is not possible for a CEO, especially in lending.”

So he denies something that, by his own account, he could not have done. It is as if a referee assured us that he had never scored a goal himself. The sentence is true and says nothing. Nobody had claimed it either: not we, and, as far as we know, not the leak.

III. Who decided

He answers the real question elsewhere, twice and with almost the same sentence. On the Russia business: the considerations had been “discussed in the competent committees and partly implemented”, and “individual clients were newly taken on”. On the clients who were on US sanctions lists: “The competent committees discussed this and decided.”

Which committees these were is in the documents we described in “We Are a Different Bank Now”. On Signa, the reputational risk committee voted five to four in November 2019. Because the required two-thirds majority was lacking, the management board decided and took Signa Prime on. On the Russia business, the US monitors had recommended in 2020 that the bank “urgently consider” exiting it altogether. In April 2021 the management board approved an expansion. In both cases the competent committee was the management board, and Sewing chairs it.

He thereby confirms our finding. The controls did not fail. They met, warned and voted, and in the end the decision was taken by the body he heads. Sewing never broke a resolution. He did not have to.

IV. What he explains

Fairness requires adding what Sewing says about his e-mails. Two lines after the call for more business, he says, there is sometimes the sentence: “But please only within the limits of our risk management.” His criticism of customer due diligence, known as KYC, was aimed at retail banking. A private customer does not need the same effort as a group with 300 subsidiaries. That is a reasonable thought. Whether the 2017 e-mail saying KYC was breaking “our revenue neck” was meant that way, we cannot verify.

What we do know is in the box Handelsblatt printed beside the interview. Sewing worked for a long time in risk management, became deputy chief risk officer and then headed internal audit. Nobody in the bank knew the controls better than he did. When such a man says of the order of sentences in his e-mails that one should “read nothing into it”, he is asking to be read less closely than he once required of his auditors.

V. What he does not say

Handelsblatt also asked about Mathias Döpfner. In 2020 Sewing had pushed internally for a loan to the Springer chief, and today the two jointly lead the initiative “Made for Germany”. Was there a conflict of interest? Sewing does not comment on names. But he says: “Either way, that surely cannot play a role five years later.” That is not a denial. The details are in “Poor for Six Days”.

Remarkable is how he speaks about the source of the leak. He calls them “whistle-blowers” and refers to the “legal protection for whistleblowers”. The same morning Gabor Steingart wrote of “data theft” in his Pioneer Briefing. The bank chief chooses his words more carefully than the journalist who defends him. More on this in “The Label”.

VI. The course

At the end of the interview Sewing speaks of record results and says the bank is “fully on course”. His last sentence reads: “But the course was always right and remains right.” In the box beside it Handelsblatt notes that since the financial crisis Deutsche Bank has paid more than 20 billion euros in fines and settlements.

Our conclusion: Sewing concedes that he is “not free of mistakes” and that one “might perhaps have prioritised differently”. Nothing more. He admits mistakes in detail and defends the direction as a whole. And the direction was what the competent committees decided under his chairmanship. The sentence “We are a different bank today” could be tested if the bank said which decision of its management board it would take differently today. Handelsblatt asked that question. The answer was the sentence about priorities.
