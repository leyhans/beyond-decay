Excellent Production Engineering

What Germany considers excellent and what China needs

In May 2025 Germany’s Excellence Commission selected 70 research clusters that the country will fund as its top research from 2026 to 2032. Production engineering is not among them. In the same year Germany lost its title as world export champion in machine tools to China.

I. A subordinate clause

RWTH Aachen is Germany’s best-known university for production engineering. On its page on the Excellence Strategy it reports three successes: a cluster for climate-friendly fuels, one for quantum computers and a new one for climate-neutral construction. Then comes a subordinate clause: “Funding for the Internet of Production Cluster of Excellence, launched in 2007, ended in 2025.”

This cluster was the heart of production research in Aachen. It began in 2006/07 as “Integrative Production Technology for High-Wage Countries” and ran from 2019 as the “Internet of Production”. It was carried by the Laboratory for Machine Tools (WZL) and by institutes from five faculties. For the period from 2026 Aachen had applied for a continuation, the “Internet of Sustainable Production”. According to RWTH’s own figures there were five applications with Aachen participation, three of them continuations, and three were approved. Sustainable production was not among them. On the university’s page this is called “ended”.

We went through the complete list of the German Research Foundation. Of the 70 clusters funded from 2026, none has production, manufacturing or machine tools in its title. The closest are computational design and construction in Stuttgart, the tactile internet in Dresden and optics in Hannover. Funding goes to quantum physics, climate, immunology, plants, brain research, mathematics, African studies and human rights. The production engineering on which Germany lives no longer appears in its top-level research funding.

II. Three readings

This can be read in three ways.

The first: the subject is exhausted. There is nothing left in production engineering where one could still become substantially better. Against this stands what was heard from the cluster itself: the question of how long, for example, the drive of a production machine lasts has not yet been answered. And China is investing billions in precisely such questions right now.

The second: the Aachen researchers did not achieve enough to justify another seven years. That is possible. The reviews are not published, and we do not speculate about their content.

The third reading concerns the yardstick. In our trilogy on “The Mediocre Excellence” we described how the competition turns excellence into a counting rule. Here it shows what this rule sorts out. Clusters of excellence are selected by international reviewers who assess scientific novelty, and novelty shows itself in publications. Quantum computers, synthetic biology and new materials produce publications. A machine tool that still works to a thousandth of a millimetre after five years produces none. It is the result of thousands of small improvements to bearings, guideways, controls and thermal compensation, and none of that fits into a top-tier journal.

How far production research itself had adapted to this yardstick is shown by the cluster. In the end it was no longer called “production engineering” but “Internet of Production”. At its centre stood the “digital shadow”, a data image of the factory. So even the production cluster had moved from the machine to the data. In the end it was not enough.

It is as if a country funded only composers of new music and no longer the instrument makers, because one cannot write papers about violins. For a while the orchestra still sounds. Then it plays on instruments from abroad.

III. The figures

The German Machine Tool Builders’ Association (VDW) presented its figures for 2025 in spring 2026.

German production fell by eight per cent in 2025 to 13.6 billion euros. Adjusted for prices it is about 35 per cent below the level of 2018. Domestic orders collapsed by 16 per cent.

China now produces 37 per cent of the world’s machine tools, Germany 12 and Japan 10. In exports China overtook Germany for the first time in 2025: 21.6 per cent of world exports against 16.7 per cent. Chinese exports rose by 18 per cent in one year. At the same time China is by far the largest market in the world.

German manufacturers react as companies react: they go where the market is. Of their production abroad, one third takes place in China.

IV. What China funds

China measures excellence differently, and explicitly so.

China’s counterpart to the Excellence Strategy is called “Double First-Class”. In its second round of 2022, mechanical engineering is a designated discipline at eleven top universities, among them the Harbin Institute of Technology, Shanghai Jiao Tong, Zhejiang, Huazhong and Xi’an Jiaotong. Control engineering follows at nine and materials science at thirty universities. The criterion is the country’s need.

From 2009 to 2020 a national major project ran solely for high-grade CNC machine tools and basic manufacturing equipment, one of sixteen major projects of the state. Universities received contracts on exactly the questions no journal is interested in: early fault detection, reliability of precision and heavy machines. According to Chinese figures, the share of domestic high-end controls in Chinese machines rose from under one to almost 32 per cent. The mean time between failures grew from 600 to over 2,000 hours.

These are not brilliant figures. In controls, spindles and ball screws China, by its own account, still lags behind. China is also self-critical: a Chinese evaluation of the programme criticises rigid guidelines and experts who pushed their own topics. It succeeded above all where it broke away from the guidelines.

But China does not stop. The 15th Five-Year Plan for 2026 to 2030 demands “decisive breakthroughs in key core technologies” for the “industrial mother machines”, as machine tools are called there. A dedicated investment fund has about 15 billion yuan in its first phase. Since 2022 the state key laboratories have been rebuilt, away from academic self-government towards institutions with a mission.

Even by the Western yardstick of publications China leads. In the Shanghai Ranking for mechanical engineering 2025, the first five include Tsinghua, Harbin, Nanyang Technological University in Singapore, MIT and Xi’an Jiaotong. RWTH is in the group of places 101 to 150.

V. The West

The USA tends to follow the German path. Its Manufacturing USA network has 17 institutes. The National Science Foundation’s “Future Manufacturing” programme funds biomanufacturing, cyber manufacturing, eco-manufacturing and quantum technology. There too the new attracts the money, the machine tool hardly.

The West has grown used to the machine simply being there. In “The Ceiling Height” we described how Europe designs chips and has them made elsewhere. “Spectators in Their Own House” and “What Remained of the Show” were about how Siemens and Schaeffler are becoming suppliers for robots that others build. Here the chain is shorter still. Whoever no longer builds the machines with which machines are built will in the end not even be a supplier.

VI. Claim and reality

RWTH describes itself as a university that sets “national and international benchmarks”. In the same year it lost the only top cluster that concerned its core, and calls that “ended”. That is where the discrepancy shows.

What follows is our conclusion, not proof. Germany awards excellence according to what reviewers consider new. That is honourable and scientifically clean. But it is no yardstick for what a country lives on. In “The Accumulated Peter Principle” we described how organisations reward what can be measured instead of what is needed. The Excellence Strategy follows the same pattern: it measures publications because they can be counted, and in doing so loses sight of the skill that cannot be counted.

China is no simple model for this. Its path costs freedom, wastes money and produces its own misdirections. But China has recognised what Germany has forgotten: the machine with which machines are built is not a closed matter. It is the basis of everything else.

In Germany production engineering is no longer considered excellent. It is merely what we live on.
