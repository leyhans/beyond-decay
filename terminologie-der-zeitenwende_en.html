The Terminology of the Zeitenwende

From the Bazooka to the Gamechanger: How the Words Grow When the Situation Shrinks

An engineer who really has something new needs no grand name for it. He shows the part. Whoever gives a project a name bigger than the project usually suspects that it will not be enough.

I. The Gamechanger

On 11 November 2026 Volkswagen stopped its “Gamechanger” project, one week before the groundbreaking. A hall for giga-casting was to be built in Wolfsburg, a process in which large sections of the car body are cast from aluminium in a single piece. Tesla has been doing this since 2020, and the Chinese manufacturers have long done it in series production. Volkswagen would have used it in Wolfsburg at the earliest in the early 2030s, for an electric Golf whose development had just been put on hold. The chair of the works council spoke of a “declaration of bankruptcy” and of a farewell “to our own claim to technological leadership”.

The project would not have brought leadership in a technology that others have mastered for six years. It would have narrowed a gap. That is honourable, but it is no gamechanger. The name promised the turn that the thing itself could not deliver.

This is not one company’s isolated case. It is a pattern of language, and politics showed the way.

II. The Dictionary

In March 2020, at the start of the pandemic, Finance Minister Olaf Scholz launched an unlimited credit programme: “This is the bazooka with which we will do what is necessary now.” A bazooka is an anti-tank weapon. In June 2020 came the stimulus package: “We want to come out of the crisis with a Wumms”, a bang. On 27 February 2022, three days after the Russian invasion of Ukraine, Scholz said as Chancellor: “We are experiencing a Zeitenwende”, a turning of the times. The word became Germany’s Word of the Year. In September 2022 came 200 billion euros against energy prices: “You could say this is a Doppelwumms”, a double bang. In December 2022, at the opening of the first liquefied gas terminal in Wilhelmshaven, he proclaimed “the new Germany speed”.

In October 2023 Defence Minister Boris Pistorius said on television: “We must become kriegstüchtig”, fit for war. A few days later the word appeared in the new Defence Policy Guidelines: “fitness for war as the guiding principle”.

In 2022 Friedrich Merz had still called the double bang a “double botch”: “Now we hear about 200 billion euros and a ‘double bang’ without knowing what it is all about.” In March 2025, before his election as Chancellor, he issued the slogan for defence: “Whatever it takes.” With that sentence Mario Draghi had saved the euro in 2012. Then came the 500-billion-euro “special fund” (Sondervermögen, literally “special assets”), the “investment booster” and the “autumn of reforms”. 2026 added the Economics Minister’s “huge data treasure” and the “Gamechanger” from Wolfsburg.

III. What the Words Do

Read as a whole, the list shows four ways of talking reality up to more than it is.

Weapons and bangs. Bazooka, bang, double bang, booster. These are words for a single moment: a shot is fired, a bang rings out, an engine starts. They say nothing about what happens afterwards. A programme called a bang need not be measured by its effect. The bang is the effect. A British publication worked out with some irony that in German politics the Wumms had become a unit of currency: one bang equals a hundred billion, a double bang two hundred.

History. Zeitenwende is a word from the calendar of epochs, from the talk of a time before and after Christ. Whoever uses it places himself beside the great ruptures of history. A turn is an event; it happens on one day. What would have been needed afterwards is work over years: procurement, personnel, procedures. The word celebrated the day and kept silent about the years. Four and a half years after the Zeitenwende, the German Council of Economic Experts asks who will pay the bill.

Possession. “Special fund” and “data treasure” turn what one does not have into something one owns. The special fund is debt. As early as August 2023 the Federal Court of Audit wrote that it would be “more accurate to speak of ‘special debts’ than of special funds”, and in January 2026 the jury of the language-critical campaign chose Sondervermögen as the Non-Word of the Year 2025, because it conceals that this is about debt. The data treasure is, as we showed in “The Treasure or the Final Sweep-Out”, above all a treasure for whoever owns the platforms on which the data sit. A word that calls debts assets spares one the question of who will repay them.

Hardness. One word on the list stands apart. Kriegstüchtig does not gloss anything over; it talks tough. Until 2023 the Bundeswehr’s goal was called the capability to defend, and the word had disappeared from the Federal Republic for good reasons. Its return is meant to attune a society to something it is not prepared for. But here too the word is bigger than the thing. An army does not become fit for war through a slogan, but through people, equipment and practice. Whether the Bundeswehr is fit for war will not be decided in a television studio.

What all four have in common is inflation. The bang is followed by the double bang, and that by “whatever it takes”, which no longer names any limit. Every crisis needs a bigger word than the last, because the last one is used up. Draghi’s sentence worked in 2012 because a central bank really can create unlimited money. An army cannot be bought without limit, as we showed in “Münchhausen’s Heirs”. When money grows faster than the factories, prices rise first.

IV. The Wonder Weapon

The pattern is old. In the summer of 1944, when the war was lost, propaganda announced the “wonder weapons” that would still turn the tide. The first was called V1, the second V2. The V stood for Vergeltung, retaliation. The name promised a turn the weapon could not bring. As we described in “From the V1 to Iron Dome”, the Volkswagen works built fuselage sections for the V1 from 1943, using forced labourers. With the V2, more people died in its production than through its use: around 20,000 prisoners in the Mittelbau-Dora concentration camp, against some 5,000 killed in the attacks.

The comparison has a limit, and we draw it clearly. The V-weapons were technically new, and their names served a criminal war. The bazooka and the gamechanger are matters of advertising language and self-deception, not propaganda for a war of annihilation. What connects them is a pattern of thought: the belief that a grand name or a technical trick can halt a structural decline. And a rule that can be read from it: the worse the situation, the bigger the word.

In his notebook on the language of the Third Reich, Victor Klemperer described how words work: “Words can be like tiny doses of arsenic: they are swallowed unnoticed, they seem to have no effect, and after a while the toxic reaction sets in after all.” The words of the Zeitenwende are not poison of that kind. But they work in the same way, unnoticed and through repetition. Whoever hears for years that the situation can be turned with a bang forgets to ask what the bang cost and what it achieved.

V. The Test

For each of these words there is a simple test, like the one an engineer applies to every component. Replace the word with what it costs and with what it has demonstrably achieved.

The bazooka was a credit line without a ceiling. The double bang was 200 billion euros of new debt for two winters. The special fund is 500 billion euros of debt over twelve years, a large part of which, according to the Council of Economic Experts, finances reshuffling within the budget. “Whatever it takes” is an exemption from the debt brake without ceiling and without end, for which, according to the same Council, up to 300 billion euros could be missing from the budget in 2040. The gamechanger would have been a foundry such as others have been running for years.

The result is not always a verdict against the thing. Some of these programmes were necessary, and the energy price brakes got many businesses through the winter. But one can only talk about them once the word has been taken away. That is exactly why we proposed a reality check in the concept of the Transparency Council: to record for every law what was promised and what actually happened.

Volkswagen has stopped its gamechanger. The game is being decided elsewhere, in plants that need no name for their foundry because they have long been casting.
